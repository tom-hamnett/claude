# Leadership coach: capability taxonomy

Version 1.0, 23 Oct 2026. Scope: what Tom *does* as a leader in recorded conversations (meetings, 1:1s, team sessions, stakeholder discussions): setting direction, deciding, driving ownership, delegating, developing people, giving feedback, influencing, handling conflict, running meetings and building trust.

**Audio-only rule.** Everything here must be assessable from the audio and transcript of Tom's conversations. Leadership work that cannot be heard (org design, written strategy documents, hiring processes, body language, follow-through that never comes up in a conversation) is deliberately out of scope. Where a behaviour is only partly audible, the "in a recording" line says what can be heard. Numbers in square brackets refer to `sources.md`. Field names in `code` are produced by `recorder/prosody.py`.

## Boundary with the Executive Presence coach

The Executive Presence (EP) rubric (`recorder/rubrics/executive_presence.md`) owns **how Tom comes across**: voice, pace, gravitas, composure, charisma, airtime as a presence signal. Its D8-D11 (listening, airtime, composure under challenge, meeting open/close) touch leadership. To avoid double-scoring:

| Overlap | EP coach scores | Leadership coach scores |
|---|---|---|
| Questions (D8) | Whether Tom sounds like he listens (follow-ups, paraphrase, liking) | Whether questions *transfer thinking and ownership* (coaching, giving the work back, surfacing dissent) |
| Airtime (D9) | Floor-holding, interruptions as dominance/prestige | Whose ideas shape the decision; whether quieter people's information reaches it |
| Challenge (D10) | Steadiness, receptiveness, vocal stress | What Tom does with the dissent: uses it, decides, disagrees-and-commits, closes the loop |
| Meeting open/close (D11) | Crispness of the spoken open and close | Whether the meeting has a decision owner, a recorded decision, owners and dates, and follow-up on last time's actions |

The leadership coach reads the same raw metrics (`talk_share`, `questions`, `interruptions`, `longest_turn_s`) but never re-scores voice or delivery; it only interprets them as leadership function.

## Capabilities (10) and sub-capabilities

Each sub-capability: **definition** / **excellent looks like** / **in a recording**.

### 1. Direction & Strategic Thinking
Making clear where we are going, why, and what matters most; and reasoning about strategy out loud so others can follow and challenge it.

- **1.1 Purpose and "why"**: Linking work to a reason people care about. / Tom names the outcome and why it matters before the task. / Purpose statements ("the reason this matters is...") precede instructions; others later repeat the purpose in their own words.
- **1.2 Priorities and focus**: Saying what matters most and what will not be done. / A short ranked list; explicit trade-offs ("we are not doing X this quarter"). / Count of explicit priorities (1-3 good, 7+ diluted); presence of "not" or "stop" choices; team questions about priority conflicts drop over time.
- **1.3 Intent and outcome clarity**: Stating the goal and the boundaries, not the method. / Specific, measurable outcome plus constraints; leaves the "how" open. / Outcome words (number, date, standard) present; "how" detail from Tom limited; others ask fewer "what do you want exactly?" questions.
- **1.4 Framing the problem aloud**: Naming what kind of problem this is (technical vs adaptive, the real question) before solving it. / "Before we solve it, what's the actual question?"; distinguishes known-fix problems from ones needing people to change. / Problem restatements, "the real question is", separation of symptoms and causes early in the discussion.
- **1.5 Thinking out loud: assumptions and trade-offs**: Exposing reasoning so it can be tested. / States facts vs assumptions vs judgements, gives probabilities, names the trade-off and second-order effects. / Phrases like "what I'm assuming is", "the bet here is", "if that's wrong then"; numeric likelihoods; invites "where's this wrong?".
- **1.6 Situation diagnosis and expectations**: Reading aloud what the context demands (start-up, turnaround, realignment, sustaining success) and agreeing expectations with the boss. / Names the situation in conversation and checks the boss sees it the same way. / Diagnostic questions in early-tenure conversations; explicit "this is a turnaround, so..." framing; expectation-setting questions in upward meetings.

### 2. Decision-Making
Getting to good, timely decisions with clear rights, real debate and committed execution.

- **2.1 Decision rights**: Making clear who decides, who inputs, who must agree. / "This is my call after hearing you" or "Priya decides; we're input". / Explicit decider statement at the start of a decision item; no repeated re-opening by others; absence = ambiguity.
- **2.2 Framing options**: Widening beyond a yes/no to real alternatives with criteria. / At least two options and stated criteria before preferences. / Count of distinct options named; "whether or not" framings flagged; criteria words ("we'll judge it on").
- **2.3 Debate quality**: Getting independent views and genuine dissent before converging. / Leader speaks last on contested calls; asks for the case against; collects views before revealing his own. / Order of opinions (does Tom state a view before others?); explicit dissent invitations; number of distinct speakers contributing to a decision item; disagreement turns present.
- **2.4 Speed and reversibility**: Matching process weight to the stakes. / Fast on reversible calls, deliberate on one-way doors; names which it is. / "Is this reversible?" language; decisions made in-meeting vs deferred; repeated deferral of the same item across recordings.
- **2.5 Closure and commitment**: Ending with a stated decision everyone commits to, including those who disagreed. / Decision restated in one sentence, dissent acknowledged, explicit commitment check. / Decision sentence present ("so we're going with..."); "can you commit to this?"; later recordings show no back-channel relitigation.
- **2.6 Calibration and learning from decisions**: Separating decision quality from outcome; using probabilities. / "Good decision, bad luck" analysis; confidence levels; decision reviews. / Probability language; post-decision reviews distinguishing process from outcome; absence of "resulting" ("it failed so it was wrong").

### 3. Accountability & Ownership
Turning talk into owned commitments, following up, and owning outcomes, including Tom's own.

- **3.1 Clear commitments (who, what, when)**: Every action has one named owner and a date. / Owner, deliverable, date, said aloud and confirmed by the owner. / Name + verb + date triplets in the last minutes; owner's verbal acceptance; actions with no owner or "we" as owner.
- **3.2 Follow-up and review**: Checking prior commitments without drama. / Opens recurring meetings by reviewing last actions; asks for status by owner. / References to previous actions at the start; "where did we get to on..."; dropped actions resurfacing.
- **3.3 Extreme ownership (leader's own)**: Owning failures in Tom's area without blaming. / "That's on me; here's what I'll change." / Ownership statements vs blame/excuse phrases; first-person singular in failure discussion.
- **3.4 Holding others accountable**: Addressing missed commitments directly and early. / Names the gap factually, asks what happened, agrees a fix and a check-in. / Missed commitments addressed in the same or next meeting vs ignored; tone remains factual (no sarcasm, no rescue).
- **3.5 Learning reviews (after-action)**: Structured debriefs after milestones or failures. / What was intended, what happened, why, what we'll do differently. / Debrief structure present; focus on process not blame; actions from the review.

### 4. Delegation & Empowerment
Pushing decisions and thinking to where the information is, at the right level of support.

- **4.1 Intent-based delegation**: Handing over the outcome and the authority, not just tasks. / "Here's the goal and the guardrails; you decide how." / Delegation statements include outcome and limits; reports say "I intend to..." rather than "can I...?"
- **4.2 Matching support to readiness**: Adjusting involvement to the person's experience with this task. / Close for new tasks, light for mastered ones; says which. / Detail of instruction relative to the person's experience; Tom explicitly naming check-in frequency.
- **4.3 Giving the work back**: Not rescuing or taking problems back. / Answers "what should I do?" with "what do you think?" when the person can solve it. / Tom's turn after a report's problem statement: solution (tell) vs question (ask); actions taken by Tom at close vs by others.
- **4.4 Autonomy support**: Offering choice, rationale and acknowledgement of perspective. / Explains why, gives options, acknowledges constraints the person faces. / Rationale statements; choice offers ("would you rather..."); low controlling language ("you must", "just do").
- **4.5 Stretch and ownership**: Giving people challenges slightly beyond their current level and real ownership. / Names the stretch and backs the person publicly. / Stretch assignments offered; others presenting their own work in meetings rather than Tom presenting it.

### 5. Coaching & Developing Others
Growing people's capability through questions, structure and attention to their growth.

- **5.1 Coaching questions**: Using open questions to help someone think. / A handful of short, open questions; stays curious longer. / Ask:tell ratio in 1:1s; open vs leading questions; length of the other person's answer after Tom's question.
- **5.2 Taming the advice reflex**: Holding back the urge to fix. / Asks before advising; offers advice as one option, labelled. / Time/turns before Tom's first recommendation; "have you thought about..." leading questions counted as advice.
- **5.3 Running effective 1:1s**: Using 1:1s as the report's meeting. / Report sets agenda; Tom listens most; covers blockers, development and wellbeing. / Report's `talk_share` in 1:1s; who raises topics; personal check-in present.
- **5.4 Career and growth conversations**: Understanding aspirations and linking work to them. / Periodic conversations on goals, strengths and next steps. / Future-oriented questions; references to the person's stated goals in later 1:1s.
- **5.5 Teaching through reasoning**: Developing judgement by sharing how Tom thinks. / Explains the reasoning behind decisions and asks the person to try the next one. / "Here's how I'd think about it" followed by handing over a similar call.

### 6. Feedback & Candour
Telling people clearly how they are doing, with care, and inviting the same back.

- **6.1 Specific praise**: Recognising what someone did and its effect. / Specific, sincere, timely; names behaviour and impact. / Praise statements with behaviour + effect vs generic "great job"; frequency relative to criticism.
- **6.2 Clear, caring criticism**: Naming a problem plainly and kindly. / Situation, behaviour, impact; no sandwich; checks understanding. / Criticism framed as observable behaviour; hedging density (`hedges_per_100w`) on the feedback turn; whether the point is actually stated.
- **6.3 Asking for feedback**: Making it easy and safe to criticise Tom. / Specific request, waits through the silence, rewards candour. / Questions like "what could I do differently?"; length of pause tolerated; Tom's response to the criticism (defend vs thank and act).
- **6.4 Timely, informal feedback**: Small, frequent feedback close to the event. / Brief comments right after meetings or moments. / Feedback referencing very recent events; low latency between event and feedback across recordings.
- **6.5 High standards with belief**: Pairing demanding feedback with explicit confidence in the person. / "I'm telling you this because the bar is high and I know you can reach it." / Standard + assurance statements together.

### 7. Influence & Stakeholder Management
Moving people Tom does not control: peers, bosses, partners, investors, other teams.

- **7.1 Interests before positions**: Uncovering what each party actually needs. / Asks "what's driving that?" and proposes options serving several interests. / Interest questions; option generation; fewer position statements repeated.
- **7.2 Stakeholder mapping and pre-wiring**: Knowing who matters and talking to them before the big meeting. / Explicit mention of who needs to be on side; 1:1 soundings before decisions. / Pre-meeting conversations recorded; references to others' known positions.
- **7.3 Managing up**: Aligning with the boss or board on expectations, style and resources. / Clear asks; no surprises; options with a recommendation. / Upward meetings contain a clear ask and recommendation; early flagging of problems.
- **7.4 Persuasion through reasons and story**: Making the case in terms the listener values. / Leads with their goals; evidence; a vivid example; addresses objections. / Audience-centred language ("for your team this means"); objections invited and answered.
- **7.5 Negotiation and calibrated questions**: Shaping agreements with questions and empathy rather than pressure. / "How would we make that work?"; labels the other side's concern; knows his walk-away. / "How/what" questions in negotiation; labels ("it sounds like..."); concessions traded not given.
- **7.6 Coalitions and reciprocity**: Building alliances through help and shared wins. / Offers help first; credits partners publicly. / Offers of help; acknowledgement of others' contribution in group settings.

### 8. Conflict & Difficult Conversations
Raising and resolving tension directly, keeping relationships intact.

- **8.1 Mining for conflict**: Bringing hidden disagreement into the open. / "I sense not everyone's convinced; who sees it differently?" / Dissent invitations; turns expressing disagreement; silent participants drawn in on contested items.
- **8.2 Safety and mutual purpose**: Keeping the other person able to talk honestly. / States shared goal; clarifies what he does and doesn't mean. / Contrasting statements ("I'm not saying X; I am saying Y"); mutual-purpose statements; signs of silence or attack from others.
- **8.3 Facts before stories**: Separating what happened from interpretation. / Opens with observable facts, offers his interpretation tentatively, asks for theirs. / Fact statements precede judgements; tentative markers ("I'm starting to think"); explicit "how do you see it?"
- **8.4 Acknowledging emotion**: Naming and validating feelings to lower temperature. / Labels emotion accurately, then moves to the issue. / Emotion labels ("sounds like this has been frustrating"); the other's next turn calmer/shorter.
- **8.5 Separating people from the problem**: Attacking the issue, not the person. / Joint problem-solving framing; no character judgements. / "We vs the problem" language; absence of "you always/never" and labels for people.
- **8.6 Courage to raise it**: Raising the hard topic early rather than avoiding it. / Names the issue in the first minutes, not the last. / Time into conversation before the difficult topic; topic raised at all vs avoided across recordings.

### 9. Meeting Leadership
Using meetings as a tool for decisions, alignment and learning (substance; EP D11 covers the spoken open and close).

- **9.1 Meeting purpose and type**: Knowing whether it is a decision, update, problem-solving or 1:1, and running it accordingly. / Purpose, output and decider stated; wrong items moved elsewhere. / Purpose and output in first two minutes; agenda items tagged by type; status updates crowding out decisions.
- **9.2 Facilitating contribution**: Making sure relevant information and views come from everyone. / Round-robins, independent views first, invites quiet experts. / Distinct speakers per item; spread of `talk_share`; Tom's `talk_share` relative to fair share; named invitations.
- **9.3 Steering to solutions**: Moving from complaint cycles to action. / Notices problem-talk loops and redirects to "what will we do?". / Ratio of solution/action talk to complaint talk; redirection statements.
- **9.4 Closing with commitments**: Recording decisions, owners, dates and what will be communicated. / Recaps decisions and who tells whom. / Decision + owner + date in last minutes; cascade agreement ("what are we telling our teams?").
- **9.5 Recurring reviews in the room**: Using the regular meeting itself as a mechanism: reviewing the same metrics and commitments each time and discussing a shared proposal rather than presenting at people. / Same standing items each week; discussion starts from questions on a pre-read, not a read-out. / Standing review items heard across recordings of the same meeting; "you've all read it, so questions first" vs Tom reading slides aloud (long Tom monologues, `longest_turn_s`).

### 10. Psychological Safety & Trust
Creating the conditions where people speak up, admit mistakes and trust Tom's word.

- **10.1 Framing work as learning**: Signalling uncertainty and the need for everyone's input. / "We haven't done this before; I need your eyes on it." / Uncertainty framing at the start; explicit request for input.
- **10.2 Acknowledging fallibility**: Admitting what Tom doesn't know and his mistakes. / Owns errors, says "I don't know", asks for help. / Fallibility statements; Tom's errors acknowledged in-meeting.
- **10.3 Responding productively to bad news**: Thanking messengers and focusing on next steps. / Appreciation, curiosity, forward focus when someone raises a problem or mistake. / Tom's turn after bad news: thanks/question vs blame/sarcasm/sigh; whether the messenger speaks again later.
- **10.4 Inviting voice**: Actively asking for concerns and ideas, especially from lower-power people. / Specific invitations; acts on input and says so. / Invitations by name; follow-through statements ("you raised X last time; we did Y").
- **10.5 Credibility and consistency**: Doing what he says he will, as heard in later conversations; explaining changes of course. / Reports back on his own actions unprompted; explains reversals. / Tom's own spoken actions reported as done in later recordings; explanations when plans change; others' references to promises not kept.
- **10.6 Care and recognition**: Showing genuine interest in people and sharing credit. / Personal check-ins; credits the team publicly; takes blame, gives credit. / Personal questions; credit statements naming people; "I" for blame and "we/they" for credit.

## Signals the coach can compute from a recording

Derived per conversation, interpreted by setting (below):

- **Decider clarity**: was a decider named for each decision item? (transcript search for "my call", "you decide", "who owns this decision").
- **Decision closure**: decision sentences present per decision item; deferred items re-appearing across recordings.
- **Commitment completeness**: share of actions with owner + deliverable + date; owner verbal acceptance; share of actions taken by Tom.
- **Ask:tell ratio**: Tom's `questions` / Tom's turns, split into open, closed and leading; for 1:1s and coaching segments.
- **Opinion order**: whether Tom states his view before or after others on contested items.
- **Dissent handling**: dissent invitations; disagreement turns by others; Tom's response (explore, decide, dismiss).
- **Ownership language**: first-person ownership vs blame/excuse phrases in failure talk.
- **Floor distribution**: Tom's `talk_share` vs fair share; number of distinct speakers per item; others' answer length after Tom's questions; `interruptions` by Tom of reports.
- **Feedback anatomy**: behaviour + impact present; `hedges_per_100w` spike on feedback turns; praise:criticism counts.
- **Response to bad news**: Tom's next turn after a problem/mistake disclosure, and whether that person speaks again.

## Context rules

- **1:1 with a report**: report should hold most of the airtime (Tom `talk_share` roughly under 0.4 as a heuristic); ask:tell high; development and blockers present.
- **Team meeting Tom chairs**: decider and output stated; Tom speaks last on contested calls; close with owners/dates.
- **Stakeholder or upward meeting**: Tom has no authority; score influence (interests, asks, recommendation) not delegation; tell-heavy is normal when presenting a recommendation.
- **Crisis or time-critical**: directive style is appropriate [10]; coach should not penalise low ask:tell if the context was explicit.
- **Founder vs corporate role**: as founder, Tom often is the decider and the bottleneck; as a corporate strategy leader he often influences without authority. The coach should tag each recording with Tom's role.
- **UK register**: understatement and hedged decisions ("I'd be minded to go with B") may be real decisions; the coach should check whether others treated it as decided before flagging ambiguity.

## Limits

Audio shows talk, not what happens between meetings; accountability and trust behaviours are scored only as far as they surface in conversation (e.g. actions reviewed in the next meeting) and need several recordings. The coach should judge trends against Tom's own baseline, avoid scoring single meetings harshly, and flag where evidence is thin. Popular leadership books are mostly practitioner wisdom; see evidence grades in `techniques.json`.
