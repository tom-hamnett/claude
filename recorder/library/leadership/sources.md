# Leadership coach: sources

Version 1.0, 23 Oct 2026. Numbers match `"ref"` in `techniques.json`. No book was read in full. "Used" says what the library actually drew on: **abstract** (journal abstract or publisher record checked during this research), **author** (author's own site, talk or article), **summary** (reputable secondary summaries and reviews; concepts as widely reported), **critique** (critical review). All technique text is paraphrased; example lines are written by us.

Credibility key: **R** = peer-reviewed empirical research; **P** = practitioner book or framework (experience-based, little or no controlled testing); **C** = consulting or company framework (internal data, not peer-reviewed).

## Books and practitioner frameworks

1. Andy Grove (1983; Vintage 1995). *High Output Management*. Used: summary (output and leverage, one-on-ones, task-relevant maturity, meetings, decision-making model of free discussion, clear decision, full support). P: operator's account from Intel; widely endorsed, untested.
2. Kim Scott (2017; rev. 2019). *Radical Candor*. St Martin's Press. https://www.radicalcandor.com. Used: author site, summary (care personally/challenge directly quadrants, get feedback first, impromptu feedback, specific praise, career conversations). P: author's Google/Apple experience.
3. Liz Wiseman with Greg McKeown (2010; rev. 2017). *Multipliers*. Harper Business. https://thewisemangroup.com. Used: author site, summary (five disciplines; accidental diminisher). P: interview data on ~150 leaders; not independently replicated.
4. Jocko Willink & Leif Babin (2015). *Extreme Ownership*; (2018) *The Dichotomy of Leadership*. St Martin's Press. https://echelonfront.com. Used: summary (extreme ownership, no bad teams, prioritise and execute, decentralised command, leading up the chain). P: military experience.
5. L. David Marquet (2012). *Turn the Ship Around!* Portfolio. https://intentbasedleadership.com. Used: author talks/interviews, summary ("I intend to", ladder of leadership, push authority to information). P: single case (USS Santa Fe).
6. Michael Bungay Stanier (2016). *The Coaching Habit*. Box of Crayons Press. https://www.mbs.works. Used: publisher blurb, summary (seven questions: kickstart, AWE, focus, foundation, lazy, strategic, learning; advice monster). P.
7. Patrick Lencioni (2002). *The Five Dysfunctions of a Team*. Jossey-Bass. https://www.tablegroup.com. Used: summary (trust, fear of conflict, mining, real-time permission, commitment, accountability, results). P: fable-based model; limited validation.
8. Patrick Lencioni (2012). *The Advantage*; (2004) *Death by Meeting*. Jossey-Bass. Used: summary (thematic goal, six critical questions, cascading communication; meeting types, tactical vs strategic). P.
9. Kerry Patterson, Joseph Grenny, Ron McMillan, Al Switzler (2002; 3rd ed. 2021). *Crucial Conversations*; (2013) *Crucial Accountability*. McGraw-Hill. https://cruciallearning.com. Used: author site, summary (make it safe, mutual purpose, contrasting, STATE, AMPP, describe the gap, motivation vs ability, who does what by when). P: vendor-backed; some company-run studies.
10. Daniel Goleman (2000). "Leadership That Gets Results." *Harvard Business Review*, Mar-Apr. Used: article text via public copy, summary (six styles, climate). C: Hay/McBer consulting data; the reported link from climate to results is not peer-reviewed. Graded contested where used for causal claims.
11. Daniel Goleman, Richard Boyatzis, Annie McKee (2002). *Primal Leadership*. HBS Press. Used: summary (resonant vs dissonant styles, visionary and coaching styles). P/C.
12. Jim Collins (2001). *Good to Great*; "Level 5 Leadership," *HBR* Jan 2001. Used: summary (Level 5, window and mirror, Stockdale paradox, stop-doing list, hedgehog). P: comparison-case design; see critique [13].
13. Phil Rosenzweig (2007). *The Halo Effect*. Free Press. Used: critique via Wharton Knowledge and reviews. Credible methodological critique of success-study books like [12].
14. Simon Sinek (2009). *Start With Why*; TED talk "How great leaders inspire action" (2009). Used: author talk, summary (Golden Circle). P: neuroscience claims are not supported; used only for the practical "lead with purpose" idea.
15. Simon Sinek (2014). *Leaders Eat Last*. Portfolio. Used: summary (circle of safety, leaders take risk first, credit). P.
16. Ronald Heifetz & Marty Linsky (2002). *Leadership on the Line*; Heifetz, Grashow, Linsky (2009) *The Practice of Adaptive Leadership*. HBS Press. Used: summary (technical vs adaptive, balcony, give the work back, productive range of distress, keep the opposition close). P: well-regarded Harvard Kennedy School teaching framework; limited empirical testing.
17. Michael Watkins (2003; updated 2013). *The First 90 Days*. HBR Press. Used: summary (STARS, five conversations with the boss, early wins, alliances). P.
18. Amy Edmondson (2018). *The Fearless Organization*. Wiley. Used: summary (set the stage, invite participation, respond productively; failure spectrum). P built on R ([19]).
19. Amy Edmondson (1999). "Psychological Safety and Learning Behavior in Work Teams." *Administrative Science Quarterly* 44(2), 350-383. Used: abstract. R: 51 teams; foundational.
20. Brené Brown (2018). *Dare to Lead*. Random House. https://brenebrown.com. Used: author site, summary (clear is kind, rumbling, BRAVING trust elements, vulnerability). P: grounded-theory qualitative research by the author.
21. Ben Horowitz (2014). *The Hard Thing About Hard Things*. Harper Business; a16z essays (e.g. one-on-ones, peacetime/wartime CEO). Used: summary, author essays. P: founder experience; directly relevant to Tom as founder.
22. Eric Schmidt, Jonathan Rosenberg, Alan Eagle (2019). *Trillion Dollar Coach*. Harper Business. Used: summary (team first, 1:1 framework, care about people, candour). P.
23. Ray Dalio (2017). *Principles*. Simon & Schuster. https://www.principles.com. Used: author site, summary (believability weighting, thoughtful disagreement, pain + reflection = progress, issue log). P: single-firm practice; radical transparency is contested.
24. Annie Duke (2018). *Thinking in Bets*. Portfolio. Used: summary (resulting, beliefs as bets, decision groups, backcasting/premortem). P drawing on R.
25. Chris Voss with Tahl Raz (2016). *Never Split the Difference*. Harper Business. Used: summary (tactical empathy, labelling, calibrated questions, accusation audit, "that's right"). P: hostage-negotiation experience; some tactics flagged for ethics.
26. Roger Fisher, William Ury, Bruce Patton (1981; 3rd ed. 2011). *Getting to Yes*. Penguin. Used: summary (separate people from problem, interests not positions, options for mutual gain, objective criteria, BATNA). P: Harvard Negotiation Project; broadly consistent with integrative-negotiation research.
27. John Kotter (1996). *Leading Change*; "Leading Change: Why Transformation Efforts Fail," *HBR* 1995. Used: summary (urgency, guiding coalition, vision communication). P; see [28].
28. Steven Appelbaum, Sally Habashy, Jean-Luc Malo, Hisham Shafiq (2012). "Back to the future: revisiting Kotter's 1996 change model." *Journal of Management Development* 31(8), 764-782. Used: abstract. R (review): support for most steps, no test of the whole model.
29. Stephen Covey (1989). *The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People*. Free Press. Used: summary (begin with the end in mind, first things first, think win-win, seek first to understand, synergise). P.
30. Marshall Goldsmith with Mark Reiter (2007). *What Got You Here Won't Get You There*. Hyperion; feedforward articles at https://marshallgoldsmith.com. Used: author site, summary (adding too much value, winning too much, passing judgement, "no, but, however", apologising, thanking; feedforward). P: executive-coaching experience.
31. Korn Ferry Leadership Architect (38 competencies, 4 factors, 12 clusters). https://www.kornferry.com/capabilities/talent-suite-hcm-software/korn-ferry-assess/leadership-architect. Used: official page, summaries (e.g. Drives vision and purpose, Ensures accountability, Balances stakeholders, Collaborates, Courage). C: used only to check taxonomy coverage.
32. Google Project Oxygen. re:Work (rework.withgoogle.com); David Garvin (2013) "How Google Sold Its Engineers on Management," *HBR*; Nextgov (Feb 2018) on the 10-behaviour update. Used: summaries of the ten manager behaviours (good coach, empowers without micromanaging, inclusive, results-oriented, good communicator, career development, clear vision, technical skill, collaborates, strong decision maker). C: internal correlational data.
33. Google Project Aristotle. re:Work guide "Understand team effectiveness," https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/understand-team-effectiveness. Used: re:Work summary (psychological safety, dependability, structure and clarity, meaning, impact). C: internal study.
34. Paul Rogers & Marcia Blenko (2006). "Who Has the D? How Clear Decision Roles Enhance Organizational Performance." *HBR*, Jan. Bain: https://www.bain.com/insights/who-has-the-d. Used: Bain page, summaries (RAPID roles). C. RAPID is a Bain trademark.
35. Amazon Leadership Principles (amazon.jobs) and Jeff Bezos, 2016 Letter to Shareholders; Colin Bryar & Bill Carr (2021) *Working Backwards*. Used: summaries (Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit; Type 1/Type 2 decisions; deciding with ~70% of information; written narratives and silent reading). C/P.

## Research papers

36. Timothy Judge & Ronald Piccolo (2004). "Transformational and transactional leadership: A meta-analytic test of their relative validity." *Journal of Applied Psychology* 89(5), 755-768. Used: abstract. R: transformational ~.44 overall validity; contingent reward ~.39 and sometimes stronger.
37. Avraham Kluger & Angelo DeNisi (1996). "The effects of feedback interventions on performance." *Psychological Bulletin* 119(2), 254-284. Used: abstract. R: average d ~.41 but over a third of interventions lowered performance; worse when attention shifts to the self.
38. Scott Tannenbaum & Christopher Cerasoli (2013). "Do team and individual debriefs enhance performance? A meta-analysis." *Human Factors* 55(1), 231-245. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23516804/. Used: abstract. R: ~20-25% improvement (d ~.67), 46 samples.
39. Peter Gollwitzer & Paschal Sheeran (2006). "Implementation intentions and goal achievement: A meta-analysis." *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology* 38, 69-119. Used: abstract via secondary sources. R: d ~.65, 94 tests.
40. Edwin Locke & Gary Latham (2002). "Building a practically useful theory of goal setting and task motivation." *American Psychologist* 57(9), 705-717. Used: summary. R: specific, difficult goals with feedback beat "do your best".
41. Karen Huang, Michael Yeomans, Alison Wood Brooks, Julia Minson, Francesca Gino (2017). "It doesn't hurt to ask: Question-asking increases liking." *JPSP* 113(3), 430-452. Used: summary (also cited in the EP rubric). R.
42. Charlan Nemeth, Keith Brown, John Rogers (2001). "Devil's advocate versus authentic dissent: stimulating quantity and quality." *European Journal of Social Psychology* 31, 707-720. Used: abstract, author profile. R: authentic dissent outperforms role-played dissent.
43. James Detert & Ethan Burris (2007). "Leadership behavior and employee voice: Is the door really open?" *Academy of Management Journal* 50(4), 869-884. Used: abstract. R: 3,149 employees, 223 managers; managerial openness more consistently related to voice.
44. Bradley Owens & David Hekman (2012). "Modeling how to grow: An inductive examination of humble leader behaviors." *AMJ* 55(4); Owens, Johnson, Mitchell (2013) *Organization Science* 24(5). Used: summaries/press releases. R (qualitative then correlational): admitting mistakes, spotlighting strengths, teachability.
45. Deborah Mitchell, J. Edward Russo, Nancy Pennington (1989). "Back to the future: Temporal perspective in the explanation of events." *Journal of Behavioral Decision Making* 2(1), 25-38; Gary Klein (2007) "Performing a project premortem," *HBR* 85(9). Used: summaries. R + P. Note: the oft-quoted "30%" refers to more reasons generated, not necessarily more correct ones.
46. Simone Kauffeld & Nale Lehmann-Willenbrock (2012). "Meetings matter: Effects of team meetings on team and organizational success." *Small Group Research* 43(2), 130-158. Used: summary (also EP rubric [58]). R: solution/action talk vs complaining.
47. Steven Rogelberg (2019). *The Surprising Science of Meetings*. Oxford University Press. Used: summaries, Wharton interview. P built on R; the claim that leaders overrate their meetings is from the author's research as summarised, not checked at source.
48. Dan Lovallo & Olivier Sibony (2010). "The case for behavioral strategy." *McKinsey Quarterly*, March. Used: article page, summary. C: survey-based; specific effect figures not verified, so none are used.
49. Garold Stasser & William Titus (1985). "Pooling of unshared information in group decision making." *JPSP* 48(6), 1467-1478. Used: summary. R: groups discuss shared information and miss unshared information (hidden profiles).
50. Irving Janis (1972; 2nd ed. 1982). *Groupthink*. Houghton Mifflin. Used: summary (leader impartiality, critical evaluator remedies). R/P: case-based; mixed later support.
51. Daniel Kahneman, Olivier Sibony, Cass Sunstein (2021). *Noise*. Little, Brown. Used: summary (decision hygiene: independent judgements, structured criteria, delay intuition). P built on R.
52. Edgar Schein (2013). *Humble Inquiry*. Berrett-Koehler. Used: summary (asking vs telling, genuine questions). P from a leading organisational psychologist.
53. Gavin Slemp, Margaret Kern, Kent Patrick, Richard Ryan (2018). "Leader autonomy support in the workplace: A meta-analytic review." *Motivation and Emotion* 42, 706-724. Used: summary. R.
54. Alison Wood Brooks, Francesca Gino, Maurice Schweitzer (2015). "Smart people ask for (my) advice." *Management Science* 61(6), 1421-1435. Used: summary. R: advice seeking raises perceived competence, especially on hard tasks and when the advisor is asked personally.
55. Robert Cialdini (1984; new and expanded ed. 2021). *Influence*. Harper Business. Used: summary (reciprocity, commitment and consistency, social proof, liking, unity). R-based P; several principles carry manipulation risk.
56. Roger Mayer, James Davis, F. David Schoorman (1995). "An integrative model of organizational trust." *Academy of Management Review* 20(3), 709-734. Used: summary. R (theory, widely supported): ability, benevolence, integrity.
57. Gregory Thompson & Robert Vecchio (2009). "Situational leadership theory: A test of three versions." *Leadership Quarterly* 20(5), 837-848. Used: summary. R: weak support for situational leadership prescriptions; basis for "contested" grading of readiness-matching.
58. Philip Tetlock & Dan Gardner (2015). *Superforecasting*. Crown. Used: summary (calibration, probabilistic language, keeping score). P built on R (Good Judgment Project).
59. David Yeager et al. (2014). "Breaking the cycle of mistrust: Wise interventions to provide critical feedback across the racial divide." *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 143(2), 804-824. Used: summary. R: feedback stating high standards and belief in the student increased revision; tested in schools, so transfer to work is inferred.
60. J. Richard Hackman (2002). *Leading Teams*. HBS Press. Used: summary (compelling direction, enabling structure, supportive context). R-based P.
61. M. Lance Frazier et al. (2017). "Psychological safety: A meta-analytic review and extension." *Personnel Psychology* 70(1), 113-165. Used: summary. R: leader relations and supportive context predict psychological safety, which relates to learning and performance.
62. Matthew Lieberman et al. (2007). "Putting feelings into words: Affect labeling disrupts amygdala activity." *Psychological Science* 18(5), 421-428. Used: summary. R: lab evidence that naming emotions dampens reactivity; applying it to labelling others' emotions in conversation is an inference.
63. Center for Creative Leadership. Situation-Behavior-Impact (SBI) feedback model. https://www.ccl.org. Used: CCL site, summaries. P: widely used, little direct testing.
64. Chip Heath & Dan Heath (2013). *Decisive*. Crown. Used: summary (WRAP; "whether or not" framing, citing Paul Nutt's research on organisational decisions). P citing R; Nutt's figures not checked at source.

## Notes on evidence grading

- **research-backed**: at least one peer-reviewed study or meta-analysis directly supports the behaviour (often outside a corporate leadership setting; transfer noted where relevant).
- **practitioner-consensus**: several independent practitioner sources recommend the same behaviour; no direct controlled test.
- **single-author**: one author or framework; plausible but unreplicated.
- **contested**: popular but with credible critiques (e.g. Level 5 [12][13], situational leadership [1][57], Goleman's style-to-results figures [10], Kotter's urgency [27][28], believability weighting [23]).
