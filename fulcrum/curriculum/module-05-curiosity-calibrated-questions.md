# Module 5 — Curiosity & Calibrated Questions

**Track 2 · Listening & connection**
**Maps to competencies:** Inquiry / Calibrated Questioning; Deep Listening (see `rubric/bars-rubric.md`)
**Grounded in:** Voss — *calibrated questions / tactical empathy*; Rackham — *SPIN questioning*; Fisher & Ury — *interests over positions*; Sofer — *curiosity & inquiry*; Bates ExPI — *Interactivity, Inclusiveness*.

> *The single highest-leverage move in any conversation is a good question. It shifts you from advocating to understanding, signals confidence (you don't need to fill the air with your own answer), makes the other person think, and surfaces the real need underneath the stated position.*

---

## 1. Why this matters

Most people, under pressure to seem impressive, **tell**. They advocate, pitch, and defend. It feels like authority — but it caps you at what you already know, and it makes the other person feel managed rather than understood.

The most influential people do the opposite: they **ask**, and then they actually listen. This is the convergence point of the whole curriculum — leadership, negotiation, and sales all independently discovered that **questions outperform statements**:
- **Negotiation** (Fisher/Ury): get past *positions* ("I need a 10% raise") to *interests* (the real driver) — and you do that by asking.
- **Tactical empathy** (Voss): *calibrated questions* ("How am I supposed to do that?") let the other person solve your problem and feel in control.
- **Consultative selling** (SPIN/Rackham): the best reps don't pitch; they ask **S**ituation → **P**roblem → **I**mplication → **N**eed-payoff questions until the buyer talks themselves into the value.
- **Sofer:** genuine curiosity ("what's alive for them?") is the doorway to connection.

Same skill, four fields. That's why it's a core module — and it's highly **measurable**, which makes it a perfect target for the diagnostic.

## 2. The core idea

A good question is **open**, **calibrated**, and **genuinely curious**.

- **Open, not closed.** Closed questions ("Do you agree?") get a yes/no and shut thinking down. Open questions ("What's your read on this?") open it up.
- **"What" and "how", not "why".** "Why did you do that?" triggers defensiveness. "What were you weighing?" / "How did you get there?" gets the same information without the threat.
- **Calibrated** (Voss): questions that gently hand the other person the problem — "How can we make this work given the deadline?" — so they engage instead of resisting.
- **Then: silence.** A question is only as good as the space you leave for the answer. Ask, then **stop talking**.

The SPIN ladder is a useful structure for any high-stakes conversation, not just sales:
1. **Situation** — understand the context ("How does this work today?").
2. **Problem** — surface difficulties ("Where does that break down?").
3. **Implication** — draw out the cost ("What does that cost you when it happens?").
4. **Need-payoff** — let *them* state the value ("What would it be worth to fix that?").

## 3. The internal mechanic (the Sofer spine)

Calibrated questions only land when the curiosity is **real**. A question asked to score a point ("Don't you think you should have...?") is heard as an attack wearing a question mark. The mechanic:

1. **Drop your answer.** Notice the urge to tell, and set it down for a moment.
2. **Get genuinely curious.** Assume there's a reason that makes sense from *their* side, and try to find it.
3. **Ask, then leave space.** Ask one clean question and let the silence do the work.
4. **Listen for the need beneath the position** (handing off to Module 6).

## 4. What good vs. poor looks like (observable)

| | Reads as low inquiry | Reads as strong inquiry |
|---|---|---|
| **Question form** | "Do you agree we should ship Friday?" (closed, leading) | "What would have to be true for Friday to work?" (open, calibrated) |
| **"Why" vs "what/how"** | "Why didn't you flag this?" | "What got in the way of flagging it earlier?" |
| **Ratio** | Talks 90%, asks ~0 real questions | Asks several genuine questions; lets answers run |
| **After asking** | Answers their own question; fills the silence | Asks, then waits — leaves a 2–3s gap |
| **Depth** | Stops at the first answer | Follows up: "Say more about that" / "What's underneath that?" |

The right-column phrases are exactly what the diagnostic detects and what the practice trains.

## 5. Practice material

> Real-world practice, not AI role-play. Self-recording challenges feed the diagnostic so you can watch your question-rate and question-quality move week over week.

**A. Observation drill (1 week).**
In conversations, silently classify each of your own contributions as a **statement** or a **question**. Just notice the ratio. Most people are shocked how rarely they ask. No need to change it yet — only see it.

**B. The "ask one layer deeper" experiment (real meeting).**
Pick one conversation. Rule: every time you'd normally give your opinion, first ask **one** open question and listen fully before responding. Bonus: at least once, follow an answer with "Say more about that." Notice how much more you learn — and how the other person warms.

**C. The SPIN ladder drill (before a real high-stakes conversation).**
Before a negotiation, pitch, or tough 1:1, write **one** question for each rung: Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff. Use them as a loose map, not a script. Afterwards note which question shifted the conversation most.

**D. Self-recording challenge (feeds the diagnostic).**
Record a meeting. Before reading the report, count your own questions and judge: how many were genuinely open vs. leading/closed? Where did you fill a silence you should have left? Then compare with the diagnostic's question-rate, open-vs-closed ratio, and post-question pause measurements.

**E. Reflection prompts.**
- Where do I tell when I could ask? What am I afraid I'll lose by asking?
- Which "why" questions could I convert to "what/how"?
- When did leaving silence after a question get me a better answer?

## 6. What the diagnostic looks for (links to rubric)

The AI evaluates **Inquiry / Calibrated Questioning** from:
- **Question rate** (questions per minute of the user's airtime) vs. baseline.
- **Open vs. closed ratio**, and **"why" vs. "what/how"** phrasing.
- **Calibrated-question markers** ("how can we…", "what would have to be true…").
- **Post-question silence** (does the user leave space, or answer their own question / fill the gap?).
- **Follow-up depth** (does the user build on answers — "say more", reflect-back — or move on?).
- **Talk-time ratio** as supporting context (advocacy-heavy vs. balanced).

And **Deep Listening** from reflect-backs/paraphrases, references to what the other person said, and minimal interruptions. See `rubric/bars-rubric.md`. As always, reported as *how you handled the conversation* — never grading the other participants.

## 7. Common traps
- **Interrogating.** A string of rapid questions feels like a deposition. Pace them; react to the answers.
- **Leading questions in disguise.** "Don't you think…?" is a statement. Make it genuinely open.
- **Asking, then talking over the answer.** The silence after the question is the skill.
- **"Why" as a reflex.** It puts people on the defensive; convert to "what/how".
