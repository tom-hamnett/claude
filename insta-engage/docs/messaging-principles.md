# DM messaging principles: The Rational Strategist

For AI-drafted, human-sent Instagram DMs to people who liked, followed or commented on a reel. British English. Plain, friendly, a little dry. Nothing that sounds like a sales sequence.

## Rules (enforce these in the drafting prompt)

1. **Length caps.** First touch ≤ 45 words. Follow-up ≤ 30. Asset share ≤ 60. Offer ≤ 70. No message longer than one phone screen.
2. **One question per message**, placed last. Never two asks.
3. **Answerable in one word.** Give 2–3 labelled options (A/B/C or short nouns) or a yes/no. Say so: "One word is plenty."
4. **Readability.** Grade 5–7 (Flesch-Kincaid). Sentences ≤ 15 words. No jargon the reader didn't use first.
5. **Personalise with what they did, not who they are.** Reference the reel they engaged with first. Mention their company only after they reply or if it is in their public bio, and say where you saw it ("your bio says...").
6. **No links, images or files in the first message.** Ask permission first ("Want me to send it?"), then send.
7. **Give an easy out.** Every ask includes a no-pressure line ("No worries if not"). Prefer no-oriented phrasing for re-engagement ("Would it be daft to...?", "Have you parked this?").
8. **Value before price.** Price is never mentioned until they've seen the free example and replied positively, or asked.
9. **Banned words:** free, exclusive, limited, opportunity, game-changer, unlock, leverage, synergy, quick call, hop on, circle back, just checking in, hope this finds you well. No em dashes. Max one emoji, never in the first line.
10. **Sound human.** Contractions, first name only if it's clearly their name, no "Dear", no sign-off block, no exclamation marks beyond one. Never claim to be a person you're not; the account is an openly anonymous persona.
11. **Cadence.** Max 2 follow-ups after no reply: day 3–4, then day 8–10. Then stop for good. Non-followers in Message Requests get one message only, ever, until accepted.
12. **Stop on any no.** "No", "not now", or silence after the last follow-up ends the sequence. Log opt-outs and never message again.
13. **Pace manually.** ≤ 20 new conversations a day while the account is small; never paste the same text twice in a row (vary at least the first line).
14. **One stage per message.** Don't thank, share and pitch in the same DM.

## Stage skeletons and examples

### Stage 1: first touch (after like, follow or comment)
Skeleton: thanks for [specific action on specific reel] + one line of context + one option-list question + "one word is fine".

> Thanks for the follow after the supply chain reel. I'm building outside-in benchmarks for people in ops roles. Which is most useful to you: cost, speed or resilience? One word is plenty.

> Cheers for the comment on the margin reel. Your bio says you're in retail ops, so I'm curious: would a quick comparison of you vs two competitors be useful, or not really your thing?

### Stage 2: share the redacted example (only after a reply)
Skeleton: acknowledge their answer + permission check (if not already given) + what it is in one line + one closed question.

> Resilience, good choice. I put together a short example comparing [Company] with [Peer A] and [Peer B]. Some bits are redacted. Want me to send it over?

> Here it is. Page 2 is the one most people look at, the lead time gap. Does it match what you see from the inside, yes or no?

### Stage 3: follow-ups (no reply)
Skeleton: no guilt, no "just checking in". Add one new small thing or a no-oriented question.

> One thing I left out: [Peer A] cut lead times by about a fifth last year. Have you parked this for now?

> Last one from me. Would it be daft to send the full version, or shall I leave it there?

### Stage 4: offer (only after a positive reply to the example)
Skeleton: tie to what they said + what the paid pack adds + price stated plainly + permission-based ask + easy out.

> Glad page 2 landed. The full pack covers [Company] and 5 peers across cost, speed and resilience, unredacted, for £199. Would that be useful, or is the sample enough for now? Either answer is fine.

> You asked about the bigger version. There are three: £99 for one metric, £249 for three, £499 with a 30-minute walkthrough. Want me to send the details for one of them?

### UK/EU compliance basics (not legal advice)
- The ICO treats private social media DMs as "electronic mail" under PECR: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/key-concepts-for-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/
- Marketing to individuals (including sole traders) by electronic mail needs consent or the soft opt-in. Instagram accounts are personal accounts, so assume an individual subscriber: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/
- Practical position: the first message is a thank-you and a question about their interests, with no promotion. Send promotional content only after they reply and ask for it (Stage 2 onward is then a response to their request). Honour any opt-out at once and keep a suppression list. Under UK GDPR, record legitimate interest for storing handle, company and notes, keep minimal data, and delete stale records. Worth a one-off check with a privacy adviser before scaling.

## What to A/B test first
Volume is small, so test one thing at a time over 40+ first touches per arm and judge on reply rate (Stage 1) and example-requested rate (Stage 2).
1. **Option list vs open question** in the first touch (e.g. "cost, speed or resilience?" vs "what are you working on?").
2. **"One word is plenty" line on vs off.**
3. **Mention company in first touch vs only after a reply** (watch for negative replies as well as reply rate).
4. **Offer wording:** price in first offer message vs "want the details?" first.

Track per message: stage, word count, variant, follower or request, replied (y/n), hours to reply, outcome.
